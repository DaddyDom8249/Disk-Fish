BUILD THE APP: PRO THROW ANALYZER

You are the lead product engineer, computer-vision engineer, sports-biomechanics engineer, UX designer, data architect, QA engineer, and technical project manager for this application.

Build a production-quality mobile-first application that analyzes a disc golfer's throwing mechanics from video and compares their measurable movement patterns against a database of professional disc golfers.

CORE PRODUCT

The core concept is:

«Record or upload a disc-golf throw → analyze the body and throwing sequence → extract measurable biomechanics → compare against relevant professional reference patterns → identify the user's largest mechanical differences → provide specific, actionable training corrections → allow the user to record another throw and measure improvement.»

The application is NOT merely a disc-speed calculator.

It is a biomechanics and technique-analysis system.

The long-term goal is to answer:

«"What exactly is my body doing during my throw, how does that differ from effective professional throwing patterns, and what should I change first?"»

1. NON-NEGOTIABLE DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

Follow these rules throughout development.

Rule 1: Build a real application

Do not create a static mockup.

All major screens, navigation, data models, analysis states, uploads, video handling, results, history, comparisons, and settings must actually function.

If a feature cannot currently be implemented, clearly isolate it behind an interface and label it unavailable rather than pretending it works.

Rule 2: No fake AI

Never generate invented measurements.

Never display:

- fake joint angles
- fake release speed
- fake spin
- fake confidence
- fake professional comparisons
- fake improvement percentages
- fake video-analysis results

Every measurement must have a provenance:

measurement
source
confidence
timestamp/frame
calculation method

If the camera cannot reliably determine a measurement, return:

Not reliably measurable from this video

instead of inventing a number.

Rule 3: Separate measured data from estimates

Every metric must have one of these classifications:

MEASURED
ESTIMATED
INFERRED
UNAVAILABLE

Examples:

Measured:

- visible joint coordinates
- frame timing
- visible body angles

Estimated:

- release angle
- body-segment velocity
- approximate disc orientation

Inferred:

- likely sequencing issue
- probable early acceleration

Unavailable:

- true disc spin if the video does not contain enough information

Rule 4: Never claim that a user should copy one professional exactly

Professional throwers have different:

- body proportions
- flexibility
- mobility
- strength
- throwing styles
- footwork
- timing
- grips
- release characteristics

The system should identify effective movement patterns and relevant reference ranges rather than forcing the user into a single professional's exact mechanics.

Rule 5: Build incrementally

Build the smallest complete vertical slice first:

VIDEO
↓
POSE DETECTION
↓
THROW PHASE DETECTION
↓
METRICS
↓
REFERENCE COMPARISON
↓
COACHING RESULT

Only expand once that pipeline works.

Do NOT build a giant architecture before proving the pipeline.

22. PRIORITIZATION ENGINE

24. COACHING OUTPUT

Every analysis should contain:

WHAT WE SAW

Plain-language description.

WHY IT MATTERS

Explain the mechanical relationship.

YOUR PRIMARY FOCUS

One thing.

WHAT TO TRY

One or two specific drills/cues.

WHAT TO WATCH FOR

A concrete visual cue.

RECHECK

Tell the user what measurement should change if the adjustment is useful.

Example:

«Your shoulders begin accelerating before your hips have completed their rotation.

Primary focus: delay active upper-body acceleration.

Try: keep the throwing arm relaxed through the plant and let the lower body initiate the rotation.

Watch for: your shoulders staying quieter until after the plant.

Recheck: hip-to-shoulder timing.»

Never present medical advice.

Avoid injury diagnosis.

If a movement appears potentially unsafe, state:

«"This analysis cannot determine whether a movement is medically safe. Consider working with a qualified throwing coach or healthcare professional if you are experiencing pain."»
